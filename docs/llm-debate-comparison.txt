SINGLE-LLM AND MULTI-LLM NB-TA-O FORMULA-DISCOVERY COMPARISON
======================================================================

Purpose
-------
This report summarizes two completed and directly comparable formula-discovery
runs. It explains what the single-LLM approach proposed and, for the multi-LLM
approach, what each model proposed in debate round 1, how it reacted in round 2,
and what it retained in its final verdict.

The report distinguishes model-generated claims from independent checks. No
formula discussed below should be interpreted as stable without numerical
evidence. Several model responses contain oxidation-state, reaction-balance, or
homologous-series errors; these are identified explicitly.

Common objective
  The models were asked to propose chemically plausible binary and ternary

PART I. SINGLE-LLM APPROACH
======================================================================

Model: GLM-4.7-Flash
--------------------

What it attempted to propose
  Binary formulas considered:
    Nb2O5, Ta2O5, NbO2, TaO2, NbO, TaO, Nb2O3, and Ta2O3.

  Ternary or solid-solution notation considered:
    (Nb,Ta)2O5, (Nb,Ta)O2, (Nb,Ta)O, (Nb,Ta)2O3, (Nb,Ta)2O7,
    (Nb,Ta)O3, and NbTaO4.

  Malformed or unresolved notation:
    (NbTa)2O5 and repeated variants of (Nb,Ta)2Nb2O5.

Chemical rationale
  The response treated +5 as the dominant oxidation state for Nb and Ta, then
  explored +4, +3, and +2 reduced oxides. It tried to motivate mixed-valence
  phases by analogy with NbO2 and Ta2O5. It mentioned rutile, rock salt or
  defect rock salt, bixbyite, pyrochlore, perovskite, tantalite, and defect
  fluorite as possible structural families.

Provisional priorities visible before the response broke down
  1. Nb/Ta pentoxide solid solutions.
  2. Dioxide and monoxide solid solutions.
  3. Reduced sesquioxide candidates.
  4. NbTaO4, although its oxidation-state and structural classification were
     not resolved coherently.

Interaction behavior and output quality
  This was a one-call mode, so there were no peers and no revision round. The
  response entered a repetitive internal-reasoning loop while trying to balance
  a mixed oxide reaction, ended mid-sentence, and never delivered the requested
  final structured answer. The stored verdict is identical to the turn rather
  than an independent synthesis. It did not produce a usable convex-hull,
  phonon, chemical-potential, or competing-phase falsification plan.

Important scientific problems
  - (Nb,Ta)2O7 would require average metal oxidation +7 for O2-, beyond normal
    Nb/Ta chemistry. The response incorrectly treated Nb2O7 and Ta2O7 as
    established targets.
  - NbTaO4 cannot have Nb(+4)/Ta(+5), because +9 does not balance four O2-.
    A simple neutral assignment is Nb(+4)/Ta(+4).
  - (Nb,Ta)2O5 is a composition-range notation. The exact 1:1 ternary formula
    is NbTaO5.
  - (NbTa)2O5, read literally as Nb2Ta2O5, is not a pentoxide composition and
    would require unusually low average cation valence.
  - Pyrochlore, perovskite, tantalite, and rutile labels were transferred too
    broadly to Nb2O5/Ta2O5 chemistry without stoichiometric justification.

Single-LLM campaign-state outcome
  The formula-policy merger produced 210 total formulas and retained 15 active
  formulas. Seven active formulas carried GLM-Flash attribution:
    Nb2O3, Nb2O5, NbO2, NbO4Ta (NbTaO4), O2Ta (TaO2), O3Ta2 (Ta2O3),
    and O5Ta2 (Ta2O5).

  The other eight active formulas came from deterministic charge-balanced
  enumeration rather than this model:
    Nb2O5Ta, Nb2O6Ta, Nb3O6Ta, NbO3Ta, NbO5Ta, NbO5Ta2,
    NbO6Ta2, and NbO6Ta3.

Interpretation
  The single-call approach provided broad brainstorming but failed to complete
  the required deliverable. Its campaign attribution mostly recovered familiar
  binary oxides and NbTaO4. It did not provide a reliable, auditable hierarchy
  of novel ternary candidates or a complete falsification strategy.


PART II. MULTI-LLM DEBATE, MODEL BY MODEL AND ROUND BY ROUND
======================================================================

1. KIMI-K2.5
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Round 1
  Proposals:
    - Binary baselines: M2O5, MO2, and MO for M = Nb or Ta.
    - Speculative Ta analogues: Ta12O29, TaO, and cluster suboxide Ta6O.
    - Ordered NbTaO5 with Nb(+5)/Ta(+5).
    - Ordered rutile-derived NbTaO4 with Nb(+4)/Ta(+4).
    - Mixed-valence Nb2Ta2O11.

  Structural rationale:
    Pentoxide block/HTB derivatives, rutile or distorted rutile, rock salt,
    Wadsley/shear structures, columbite-like or dense monoclinic NbTaO5, and
    ordered-rutile or trirutile NbTaO4.

  Proposed tests:
    Formation energies and phonons for binaries; cluster expansion and an
    order-disorder temperature for NbTaO5; mixing enthalpy for NbTaO4; a
    ternary convex hull for Nb2Ta2O11; and oxygen-chemical-potential analysis.

  Peer-facing stance:
    Challenged ideal-solution assumptions, transfer of Nb suboxide chemistry
    directly to Ta, and stability claims made without calculations.

Round 2
  Revisions:
    - Downgraded ordered NbTaO5 because T-Nb2O5 and beta-Ta2O5 have incompatible
      topologies and configurational entropy may favor disorder.
    - Replaced Nb2Ta2O11 with Nb2Ta2O9 and Nb8Ta2O25 as proposed shear targets.
    - Recast NbTaO4 as a high-pressure CaCl2-type or baddeleyite-related target.
    - Retained (Nb,Ta)O only as an extreme high-pressure possibility.

  New tests:
    Interface/phase-separation energies for NbTaO5; cluster expansion for shear
    candidates; pressure-dependent enthalpy near 10 GPa; an equation of state
    for TaO; and grand-potential phase diagrams at 0, 10, and 20 GPa.

  Debate response:
    Criticized peers for neglecting entropy, assuming T-Nb2O5 ordering, and
    treating NbTa11O29 automatically as a distinct line compound rather than a
    possible limited solid solution.

Final verdict
  High priority:
    (Nb,Ta)O2 rutile solid solutions and dilute cross-doping of Nb2O5/Ta2O5.

  Moderate priority:
    Reduced shear solid solutions, including Nb12-xTaxO29, NbTa11O29, and
    Nb2Ta2O9; plus high-pressure NbTaO4 if quenchable.

  Low priority:
    Ambient ordered NbTaO5 and (Nb,Ta)O.

  Excluded:
    Columbite-type NbTaO5 and ilmenite-type NbTa2O6.

  Audit notes:
    Nb2Ta2O11 requires average metal oxidation +5.5 and is not viable under the
    stated assumptions. Nb2Ta2O9 is M4O9, not the n=5 M_nO_(2n-1) member.
    Nb8Ta2O25 reduces to an M2O5 composition and is not the claimed n=13
    mixed-valence Magneli formula. Several proposed reactions were unbalanced.


2. GLM-4.7
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Round 1
  Proposals and filtering:
    - Accepted M2O5, MO2, and MO as binary baselines.
    - Deprioritized reduced Ta-rich Ta6O and Ta12O29.
    - Retained NbTaO5 only as a speculative H-/T-Nb2O5 block derivative;
      explicitly rejected columbite AB2O6 as a formula mismatch.
    - Rejected ordered NbTaO4 as an ambient line compound but retained the
      x = 0.5 composition of a rutile (Nb,Ta)O2 solid solution.
    - Considered high-pressure baddeleyite NbTaO4.
    - Rejected Nb2Ta2O11 as a Magneli composition.
    - Introduced NbTa11O29 as its preferred shear candidate.

  Tests and criteria:
    Cluster expansion for NbTaO5; ordered-cell versus SQS calculations for
    NbTaO4; convex hulls, phonons, vacancy energies, oxygen chemical potential,
    and pressure calculations. It proposed approximate 25 meV/atom ordering or
    tie-line thresholds, which are screening heuristics rather than proofs.

  Peer interaction:
    Supported skepticism about Ta suboxides and directly challenged columbite
    NbTaO5, ordered-rutile NbTaO4, and Nb2Ta2O11.

Round 2
  Revision:
    Raised ordered NbTaO5 from entropy-disfavored speculation to a campaign
    target. It argued that Ta(+5) and Nb(+5) may select different coordination
    sites in low-symmetry T-Nb2O5 because of 5d/4d bonding differences.

  Retained:
    Rutile-solid-solution NbTaO4 and shear candidate NbTa11O29.

  Added:
    Highly speculative Nb3TaO9 as a block homologue. Other brainstormed formulas
    were considered and deferred or rejected.

  Debate response:
    Challenged radius-only arguments against ordering and argued that electronic
    site preferences could matter. It retained disorder as the rutile null
    hypothesis while broadening low-symmetry and metastable searches.

Final verdict
  1. High confidence: NbTaO4 only as a rutile solid-solution composition.
  2. Medium: NbTa11O29 as a reduction-synthesis target.
  3. Low to medium: ordered T-Nb2O5-derived NbTaO5, especially as a metastable
     thin-film target.
  4. Speculative: high-pressure CaCl2-type NbTaO4.
  5. Excluded: columbite NbTaO5 and Ta-rich suboxides.

  Audit notes:
    NbTa11O29 has average metal oxidation +4.833, but it is not M_nO_(2n-1):
    M12O_(2*12-1) is M12O23, not M12O29. If all eleven Ta atoms are +5, Nb must
    be +3. Nb3TaO9 requires average +4.5 and is M4O9, not M3O7.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

  ordered-rutile NbTaO4, Nb2Ta2O11, and low-valent NbTaO3.

  The response then entered recursive repetition and ended before delivering a
  complete argument, oxidation table, falsification protocol, or ranked set.
  Repeated text should not be counted as independent support.

Round 2
  Again produced a planning document rather than a clean answer. It considered
  NbO, NbO2, Nb2O5, TaO2, Ta2O5, and high-pressure TaO. It planned to retain
  ordered T-Nb2O5-derived NbTaO5, rutile-solid-solution NbTaO4, and NbTa11O29.
  It floated Nb3TaO9, NbTa3O9, Nb5Ta5O16, Nb25TaO63, Nb2TaO7, NbTa2O7, and
  Nb3TaO7 without developing consistent charge or structural rationales.

  It supported peers' rejection of columbite, ordered rutile, Nb2Ta2O11, and
  Ta-rich suboxides, while challenging radius-only arguments. The promised
  site-preference DFT and hull protocol was never assembled into a final answer.

Final verdict
  Ranked rutile-solid-solution NbTaO4 as "confirmed," called ordered NbTaO5 and
  NbTa11O29 the strongest discovery targets, and listed Nb3TaO9 and Nb25Ta5O63
  as speculative. It recommended substitution energies, diffraction searches,
  convex hulls, and kinetic barriers.

  Audit notes:
    "Confirmed" is unsupported by this debate. Both rounds were incomplete,
    and the verdict introduced or strengthened candidates not coherently
    developed in its own turns. It inherited the incorrect NbTa11O29 Magneli
    classification and inconsistent formula arithmetic.

PART IV. CROSS-MODEL CONVERGENCE AND DISAGREEMENT
======================================================================

Strongest convergence
  1. NbTaO4 should first be treated as the x = 0.5 composition of a rutile
     (Nb,Ta)O2 solid solution, not assumed to be a distinct ordered compound.
  2. Columbite is a poor direct assignment for NbTaO5 because the conventional
     columbite stoichiometry is AB2O6 rather than ABO5.
  3. Nb2Ta2O11 is not charge-balanced under ordinary Nb/Ta oxidation states and
     should be rejected under the stated assumptions.
  4. Ordered NbTaO5 remains a testable but low-confidence hypothesis. The main
     question is whether low-symmetry coordination preferences overcome mixing
     entropy and phase separation.
  5. Reduced shear/block chemistry deserves exploration, but exact formulas and
     homologous-series assignments require independent arithmetic checks.
  6. Pressure phases and low-valent Ta compounds should remain explicitly
     speculative until enthalpy, dynamics, and recoverability are computed.

Major disagreements or revisions
  - Several models began by emphasizing entropy and similar ionic radii, then
    revised toward possible electronic site ordering in T-Nb2O5-derived NbTaO5.
  - Pressure NbTaO4 moved among baddeleyite and CaCl2-type descriptions without
    numerical evidence selecting either family.
  - NbTa11O29 was repeatedly treated as a central shear target, but models did
    not resolve whether it represents a line compound, limited Ta/Nb solubility,
    or even the intended substitution direction.
  - The two Gemma models supplied highly correlated responses, and GLM-Flash
    supplied incomplete/repetitive responses. Seven endpoints therefore did not
    yield seven fully independent scientific analyses.


PART V. RECOMMENDED INTERPRETATION OF THE TWO APPROACHES
======================================================================

Single-LLM approach
  Strengths:
    Fast, inexpensive, and broad enough to recover familiar binary oxide and
    solid-solution concepts.

  Weaknesses observed in this run:
    The single response failed to terminate cleanly, had no peer correction,
    reused the same text as its verdict, and did not deliver the required
    falsification plan. Erroneous formulas and family assignments remained
    unchallenged.

Multi-LLM debate approach
  Strengths:
    Produced explicit criticism, round-to-round revisions, confidence tiers,
    and a much richer computational validation agenda. The panel converged on a
    defensible null hypothesis for NbTaO4 and rejected several clear formula or
    prototype mismatches.

  Weaknesses observed in this run:
    Models copied or strongly echoed one another, repeated the same arithmetic
    errors, and sometimes converted speculative statements into apparent
    consensus. Debate improves scrutiny but does not replace deterministic
    charge-balance, stoichiometry, reaction-balance, or structure-family checks.

Best scientifically defensible campaign sequence from the collected text
  1. Establish binary and solid-solution controls:
       Nb2O5, Ta2O5, NbO2, TaO2, and selected (Nb,Ta)O2 and pentoxide mixtures.

  2. Test ordered NbTaO5 against disorder and phase separation:
       enumerate low-symmetry sites, compare ordered cells with SQS/cluster
       expansion, calculate the ternary hull, and evaluate finite-temperature
       configurational effects.

  3. Rebuild the reduced shear candidate list deterministically:
       generate valid M_nO_(2n-1) and Wadsley-Roth compositions first, then
       substitute Nb/Ta while enforcing charge balance. Do not inherit the
       transcript's n-index assignments without correction.

  4. Treat pressure NbTaO4 and low-valent Ta oxides as separate hypotheses:
       compute pressure-dependent enthalpies, phonons, decomposition paths, and
       reverse barriers before discussing recoverability.

  5. Require every LLM proposal to pass deterministic prechecks:
       canonical formula parsing, oxidation-state feasibility, balanced
       reactions, prototype stoichiometry compatibility, and duplicate-family
       detection before MLIP or DFT resources are allocated.


FINAL COMPARISON
======================================================================

The single model mainly supplied an incomplete inventory. The debate panel
supplied a scientific process: proposals were challenged, revised, ranked, and
paired with calculations that could reject them. Its most robust conclusion was
not a novel stable compound, but a better ordering of hypotheses:

  - First: rutile and pentoxide solid-solution controls.
  - Second: corrected reduced shear/block candidates.
  - Third: ordered NbTaO5 as a low-confidence, falsifiable hypothesis.
  - Fourth: high-pressure NbTaO4 and low-valent Ta chemistry.

The multi-LLM process was therefore more useful than the single-call output, but
its consensus is not validation. The repeated formula arithmetic errors show why
